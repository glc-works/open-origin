Most cocoa in Indonesia is sold simply dried, with little or no fermentation [1]. In Jembrana, in the west of Bali, farmers took the longer road. Fermentation in wooden boxes, done by farmers and at their cooperative, is what develops the flavour that chocolate makers look for.

The cooperative is Koperasi Kerta Semaya Samaniya, KSS for short. From 2011 it was supported by the foundation Yayasan Kalimajari, led by I Gusti Ayu Agung Widiastuti. Its members are organised in *subak abian*, the customary dryland farmer groups of Bali: 609 farmers in 38 groups in 2018. The cooperative held both UTZ and USDA Organic certification, and its cocoa reached the top 50 of 166 samples at the Cocoa of Excellence awards in 2017 [2].

The French chocolate maker Valrhona first met the cooperative in 2013, received its first shipment in 2015 and signed a five-year agreement in 2023 [1]. Today the cooperative ferments around 50 tonnes of beans a year and exports to France, the Netherlands, Belgium and Japan [3].

Member counts differ between sources and years: 609 farmers in 2018 [2], 409 producers on Valrhona's page [1].
