Sumbawa's forest honey comes from *Apis dorsata*, the giant honeybee, called *aning* locally. It cannot be kept in hives: it builds its open combs high in forest trees, and people climb to harvest them [1].

Harvesters around Sumbawa Besar and the Batulanteh hills work together in the Sumbawa forest honey network, Jaringan Madu Hutan Sumbawa (JMHS). They follow a sustainable harvest method: only ripe honey is taken, about a quarter of each comb is left for the colony, and the honey is drained by dripping (*tiris*) rather than squeezed by hand. The village of Batudulang has become a learning centre for *Apis dorsata* honey, visited by delegations from the Philippines [1].

The harvest runs from April to November; December to March is the lean season [1]. "Madu Sumbawa" was registered as a Geographical Indication in 2011 [2].
