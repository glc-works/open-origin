Ethiopia is the home of *Coffea arabica*: the plant grows wild in the highlands of the south-west, and the country is still Africa's largest coffee producer. More than four million smallholders grow it, nearly all the work is still done by hand, and about half the harvest is drunk at home in Ethiopia [1].

Yirgacheffe is a town of about 1,900 metres in the Gedeo Zone, on the eastern escarpment of the Ethiopian Highlands, and the name of the coffee from around it [2][3]. Most of it is garden coffee: trees planted around the homestead rather than on plantations [1]. The Gedeo people farm in layers of trees, crops and coffee, and because of this agroforestry and its cultural importance, the Gedeo Cultural Landscape was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023 [2].

Much of it reaches buyers through cooperatives that run their own washing stations. The largest, the Yirgacheffe Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union, was founded in 2002 and sells both washed and sun-dried coffee [4][5]. The Gedeo Zone has about 62,000 hectares of coffee gardens [5].

Ethiopia owns the trademarked regional name Yirgacheffe, along with Harar, Limu, Jimma and Kaffa [1]. In 2006 it became the centre of a dispute when Oxfam accused Starbucks of opposing Ethiopia's US trademark applications for Sidamo, Harar and Yirgacheffe [1].
