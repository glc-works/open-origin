Sidama is named after the Sidama people, their language and their land, north-east of Lake Abaya and south-east of Lake Hawassa. Long a zone of the Southern Nations region, it became a regional state of its own on 18 June 2020, after 98.5% voted for self-rule in the 2019 referendum. Its capital is Hawassa [1].

Most people here are farmers, and coffee is the region's main cash crop. Sidama supplies over 40% of the washed coffee that reaches Ethiopia's central market [1]. The coffee grows between 1,500 and 2,200 metres and is known for berries and citrus, above all lemon, with a bright acidity. In the older trade sense, the name Sidamo also covered the coffees of Yirgacheffe and Guji to the south [2].

Coffee prices swing, and when they fall many households here go back to growing food for themselves [1]. Like Yirgacheffe and Harar, Sidamo is one of the regional coffee names Ethiopia tried to register as a trademark in the United States [2].
