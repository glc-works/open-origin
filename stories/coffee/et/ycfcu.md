The Yirgacheffe Coffee Farmers Cooperative Union (YCFCU) was founded in 2002, four years before the name Yirgacheffe first appeared on a bag of coffee at Starbucks [1]. Its member cooperatives are all in the Gedeo Zone, and their farmers are Gedeo people [2]. The union produces washed Yirgacheffe and Sidamo coffee as well as sun-dried coffee, and by 2010 all its member cooperatives were certified Organic and Fair Trade [1].

The union acts as the marketing body for its cooperatives: it arranges loans so they can buy cherries from members, and shares the profit back with them [2]. For years it had to rent the government's only processing plant in Addis Ababa and turn buyers away. In 2014 its cooperatives produced about 40,000 tonnes of coffee, but the union exported only 2,000 tonnes. In 2015 it opened its own warehouse and processing plant in Addis Ababa and expected to double exports [1]. It has also built a school in Dila and bridges to reach isolated areas [2].

This story was written from what the union and its partners have published. If you work at YCFCU, you are welcome to tell it in your own words.

### Where sources differ

- Size of the union: **26 cooperatives with over 43,000 farmers** according to ACDI/VOCA in 2015 [1], **28 cooperatives with 43 washing stations and over 38,000 farmers** according to the union's seller profile on Algrano [2].
