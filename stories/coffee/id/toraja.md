In the land of the tongkonan, the traditional ancestral houses, coffee grows among rocky hills and terraced rice fields. Coffee from this area used to be traded through the town of Kalosi in Enrekang, so the two names are often used interchangeably.

Toraja coffee faded for a time. In the 1970s a Japanese company rebuilt farms and processing there, and Toraja has been a favourite in Japanese cafés ever since.

Its taste bridges Sumatra and Java: a heavy body like Sumatra, but cleaner and sweeter, with touches of spice and dark berries.
