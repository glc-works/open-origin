This is where the story of Indonesian coffee begins. In 1696 the Dutch governor in Malabar, India, sent arabica seedlings to Batavia. A flood destroyed the first ones, but a second shipment in 1699 survived [1]. Java coffee soon dominated the Amsterdam auctions, and "java" became another word for coffee [2].

Behind that fame is a bitter history. From 1830 to around 1870, under the Cultivation System, farmers in the Preanger (Priangan) region of West Java and elsewhere were forced to grow coffee and deliver it to government warehouses instead of paying taxes [1][2].

Priangan coffee has woken up again. Kopi Arabika Java Preanger received a Geographical Indication in 2013 [4], and in 2016 a coffee from Mount Puntang in Bandung Regency was named the best at the Specialty Coffee Association of America's expo in Atlanta [3].
