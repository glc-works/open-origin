Nyeri County lies on the south-west flank of Mount Kenya, with the town of Nyeri as its capital [2]. The high plateaus around Mount Kenya and the Aberdare Range are among the country's main coffee regions [1].

Kenyan coffee is known for its cooperative system, which covers growing, processing, milling, marketing and the auction [1]. About 70% of it comes from small farms, and around 150,000 farmers grew coffee in 2012 [1]. In Nyeri, many cooperative societies run several "factories" each: the Kenyan name for a washing station, where members bring their cherries [1]. Coffee is auctioned at the Nairobi Coffee Exchange, where US$127.8 million worth was sold in the 2022–23 coffee year [1].

Nyeri Hill, an estate owned by the Catholic Church and one of the oldest coffee farms in Kenya, shows how the coffee is made. It grows SL28, SL34 and Ruiru 11 at about 1,700 metres in red volcanic soil, shaded by macadamia trees. All of it is fully washed: the cherries are pulped, dry-fermented for 24 hours, washed in river water and dried on raised beds [3].

### Where sources differ

- When coffee came to Kenya: **1893**, brought by French Holy Ghost Fathers from Réunion, according to one source; **about 1900**, by the British, according to another. Both are cited in Wikipedia's article [1].
