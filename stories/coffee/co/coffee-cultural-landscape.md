The Eje Cafetero, or Coffee Axis, lies in the central Andes across the departments of Caldas, Risaralda and Quindío, with parts of Tolima, Valle del Cauca and Antioquia. Its main cities are Manizales, Pereira and Armenia [1].

In 2011, eighteen towns across six sites in the region, with the coffee farms around them, were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the Coffee Cultural Landscape. They keep several traditional ways of growing coffee, along with the culture and festivals that grew up around it [1]. In 1999 an earthquake of magnitude 6.4 struck the region hard, but its economy recovered quickly [1].

Colombian coffee is mostly grown on small family farms. Since 1927 they have been organised in the National Federation of Coffee Growers, which today represents more than 500,000 producers, funds research and checks export quality. In 1981 the federation created the Juan Valdez brand to set 100% Colombian coffee apart from blends [2].

Coffee came slowly at first: farmers resisted it because a new tree takes about five years to give its first harvest [1].
