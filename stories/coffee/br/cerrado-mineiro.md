The Cerrado Mineiro is the savanna country of western Minas Gerais: the Alto Paranaíba, the Triângulo Mineiro and the northwest of the state. Coffee is young here. In the late 1960s, after pests ravaged farms in Paraná and São Paulo, government programmes paid growers to open new plantings on the Cerrado, and farmers from those states moved in. The soil is naturally poor and had to be corrected and managed for years before it suited coffee [2].

Today the region covers 55 municipalities, about 4,500 growers and some 234,000 hectares of coffee, producing around 6 million bags in an average year. The town of Patrocínio alone has 52,000 hectares of coffee [1][2]. Coffee grows between 800 and 1,300 metres, with hot, wet summers and mild, dry winters [1][3].

In 2005 the region became the first in Brazil to receive a geographical indication for coffee, and on 31 December 2013 it became the first Brazilian coffee region with a Denomination of Origin, which allows only arabica [1][2][3]. Only a small share of the harvest carries the seal: the coffee must come from above 800 metres, pass a quality check and be stored in an approved warehouse. Each sealed lot can be traced by QR code back to the farm, the producer, the altitude and coordinates of the plot, and the harvest year [2].

Like most Brazilian coffee, it is usually dried in the whole cherry, in the sun, in one big dry-season harvest [4].

### Where sources differ

- Size of the region: **about 3,500 growers on 147,000 hectares** when the Denomination of Origin was granted [3], **about 4,500 growers on 234,000 hectares** in 2025 [1]. The difference is mostly growth over time.
