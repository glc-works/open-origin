In the Menoreh hills of Kulon Progo, west of Yogyakarta, coconut sugar starts with a climb. Tappers cut the flower stalk of the coconut palm and hang a container to catch the sap, which collects for about twelve hours. A little slaked lime or sodium bisulfite slows the sap from fermenting before it is cooked [1].

About a fifth of the sap's weight ends up as sugar, so a kilogram of sugar needs a day's sap from three or four trees [1]. Much of it is made into *gula semut*, "ant sugar", a fine granulated form, by methods handed down in families in villages such as Hargowilis in Kokap [2].

"Gula Kelapa Kulonprogo Jogja" was registered as a Geographical Indication in Indonesia in 2014 [4], and in October 2025 it also received recognition in the European Union [3].
