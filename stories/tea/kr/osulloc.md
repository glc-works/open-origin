O'Sulloc was founded in 1979 by Suh Sung-hwan, who wanted to revive Korean tea on Jeju land that was then uncultivated. Its first fields, Dosun, were planted on the slopes of Hallasan in 1980; Seogwang and Hannam on Jeju, and Gangjin on the mainland, followed. The company has been a subsidiary of Amorepacific since 2020 [1].

The fields cover about 330 hectares, of which about 180 are harvested. They are certified organic, including under the USDA's National Organic Program since 2010, and all the tea is planted, tended and harvested by machine [1]. The first green teas, Mansu, Cheonsu and Baeksu, came out in 1980, and *ujeon* leaf tea in 1992 [1].

In 2001 the company opened a tea museum at the Seogwang fields, and in 2005 a tea research institute next to them [1].
