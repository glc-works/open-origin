The Wuyi Mountains rise in northern Fujian on the border with Jiangxi, from about 200 metres in the valleys to 2,158 metres at Mount Huanggang. In the east, red sandstone cliffs with flat tops stand over winding streams. The range has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999 [2].

Wuyi gave the English language one of its first words for tea: "Bohea", from the local pronunciation of Wuyi, once meant any darker tea [1]. In the 16th century Wuyi tea makers learned to pan-fire leaves from monks of Songluo Mountain, then found that letting the leaves partly oxidise first made a darker, fragrant tea: oolong. Black tea was most likely invented here too, and Lapsang souchong may be the first black tea ever made [1].

Wuyi oolongs are well oxidised, twisted into thin strips and fired heavily, with a smoky taste and notes of stone fruit [1]. Bushes on the rocky slopes give less, so the tea costs more, and leaves from old bushes cost most of all [1].

The most famous is Da Hong Pao, "Big Red Robe". Only six mother bushes remain on a cliff at Jiulongyu. In 2006 the city government insured them for 100 million yuan and banned private picking from them; the Da Hong Pao sold today comes from cuttings [3]. Every spring a "Mountain Call" ceremony in the old imperial tea garden calls the tea to sprout [2].
