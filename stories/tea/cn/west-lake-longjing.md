Longjing, "Dragon Well", is a pan-fired green tea named after a historic well near Longjing village, by West Lake in Hangzhou. It is counted among China's Famous Teas [1].

Tea from the traditional area around West Lake is protected as a geographical indication in China and in the European Union. Stricter definitions keep the name for the villages and plantations around the lake, or Xihu District as a whole; looser ones allow any Longjing from Zhejiang. Much tea sold as Longjing is made elsewhere, even as far as Yunnan, Guizhou and Sichuan [1]. Growers rank the five peaks around the lake as Lion, Dragon, Cloud, Tiger and Plum Flower, with Lion Peak (Shi Feng) the most sought after [1].

The leaves are fired in a pan and shaped flat by hand while they fire. The tea is yellow-green in the cup, sweet, fresh and often chestnut-like. The finest grades, *mingqian*, are picked before the Qingming festival in early April [1].

During the Qing dynasty Longjing became an imperial tribute tea. Legend says the Qianlong Emperor gave imperial status to eighteen bushes near West Lake, whose tea is still prized [1].
