The Malabar estate lies on the slopes of Mount Malabar near Pangalengan, south of Bandung, at 1,400 to 1,600 metres [4]. It covers more than 2,000 hectares and once ran two factories [3]. The estate is still run by the state plantation company PTPN [1].

Its best-known manager was Karel Albert Rudolf Bosscha, who ran it until his death there in 1928. He opened a free school for workers' children in 1901, funded the Bosscha Observatory and helped found the technical college that is now ITB [1]. He asked to be buried among the tea bushes near his house in Banjarsari, now a heritage site [3].

Pickers take only the bud and the top two leaves, by hand. The leaf is made into fully oxidised black tea, and in 2025 the estate made its first export of a premium leaf grade, to Taiwan [4]. Tea from the Priangan highlands carries the Geographical Indication "Teh Java Preanger", registered in 2015 [5].

### Where sources differ

- When Bosscha took over the estate: **1896** according to Wikipedia's article on Bosscha [1], **1886** according to its article on Malabar [2].
