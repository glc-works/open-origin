Until the middle of the nineteenth century, the small Banda Islands in Maluku were the world's only source of nutmeg and mace, both from the tree *Myristica fragrans* [1].

That made them a prize. In 1621 the Dutch East India Company under Jan Pieterszoon Coen conquered the islands; only about 1,000 Bandanese survived. Coen divided the nutmeg groves into 68 parcels of about 1.2 hectares and handed them to Dutch planters, the *perkeniers* [1]. Rival English claims to the island of Run were settled in the 1667 Treaty of Breda, in which Run was exchanged for Manhattan. The monopoly ended when the British carried seedlings to Ceylon and Singapore in 1810 [1].

Nutmeg is still the islands' living. In 2018, 1,211 families grew it on 382 hectares and harvested 922.5 tonnes. Growers pick three to four times a year and dry the nuts in the sun by day and over a fire into the evening, for up to a month [2]. "Banda Island Nutmeg" was registered as a Geographical Indication in 2019 [3].
