BP 534 was selected by the Indonesian Coffee and Cocoa Research Institute (ICCRI) from a single tree, number 6, in a robusta plantation in Jember, East Java. [1]

Like other robusta clones, BP 534 cannot pollinate itself: it **must be planted together with other clones** for the cherries to set. It starts bearing in the second year and suits agroforestry, with coffee grown under shade trees. It is highly resistant to leaf rust but susceptible to the root-lesion nematode *Pratylenchus coffeae*. [1]

**Yield and fit:** about 1,700 to 2,200 kg of green coffee per hectare, with a cherry-to-green-bean outturn of about 21%. It does best in wet climates between 400 and 900 m. It is the clone Indonesian farmers grow most. [1]
