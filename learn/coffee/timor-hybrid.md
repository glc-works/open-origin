On the island of Timor in the 1920s, an arabica tree and a robusta tree crossed naturally. The offspring behaved like arabica, with the right number of chromosomes and self-pollination, but carried robusta genes for resistance to coffee leaf rust.

The Timor Hybrid became the parent of most rust-resistant arabica varieties grown today, including Catimor and Sarchimor types. Its own cup can be good, though sometimes less sweet and clean than pure arabica.

**In Indonesia:** called *Tim Tim* by farmers, especially in Aceh (Gayo) and North Sumatra, where it is still widely planted.
