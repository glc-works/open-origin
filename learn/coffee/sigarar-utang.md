Sigarar Utang was found in 1988 in a farmer's field in Batu Gajah village, Paranginan, Humbang Hasundutan (Lintong), North Sumatra, at about 1,400 m. [1] It is believed to be a natural cross, but sources differ on the parents:

- **2018**, the Ministry of Agriculture's variety guide (Randriani & Dani, *Pengenalan Varietas Unggul Kopi*, IAARD Press, 2018) [1]: probably the Typica line BLP crossed with Catimor.
- **2025**, Royal Coffee's survey of Indonesian cultivars (Evan Gilman, 2025) [2]: genetic tests point to Typica (Blawan-Pasoemah) crossed with the Timor Hybrid.

The two are close, since Catimor itself descends from the Timor Hybrid.

The Ministry endorsed it as a variety in 2005. [2]

The guide gives an average of 1.5 t of green coffee per hectare (0.8 to 2.3 t) at 1,600 trees per hectare, and recommends it above 1,000 m. It is somewhat resistant to leaf rust but susceptible to the nematode *Radopholus similis*. [1]

Farmers like it because it starts producing quickly and bears through much of the year, which helps cash flow. That is where the name comes from: in Batak, *sigarar utang* means roughly "the one that pays off debts".

**In Indonesia:** common around Lake Toba, Lintong and Mandailing, and planted in West Java and other regions too.
