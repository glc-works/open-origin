---
title: Natural
crop: coffee
type: process
summary: Whole cherries are dried in the sun with the fruit still on, giving a sweet, fruity, heavier cup.
---

The natural (or "dry") process is the oldest way to process coffee. Whole ripe cherries are spread on patios or raised beds and dried in the sun for two to four weeks, turned often so they dry evenly and do not mould. The dried fruit is then hulled off the bean.

Because the bean dries inside the fruit, it takes on sweetness and fruit flavour: berries, tropical fruit, wine. It needs little water but a lot of attention, and uneven drying can produce off-flavours.

**In Indonesia:** called *olah kering* or *natural*. Robusta has long been dried this way, often without sorting. A newer generation of arabica farmers in Gayo, Java and elsewhere use careful natural processing for specialty lots.
