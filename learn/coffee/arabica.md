---
title: Arabica
crop: coffee
type: species
latin: Coffea arabica
summary: The most widely grown coffee species, prized for sweetness and acidity, from the highlands of Ethiopia and South Sudan.
---

Arabica is a natural hybrid of two other coffee species, *Coffea canephora* and *Coffea eugenioides*. That makes it tetraploid, with four sets of chromosomes, and unlike most coffee species it pollinates itself. Its wild home is the forest of southwestern Ethiopia and the Boma Plateau of South Sudan.

It accounts for roughly 55–60% of the world's coffee. It grows best between about 1,000 and 2,200 metres, where cool nights slow the ripening of the cherry and build sugar and acidity. That is why most specialty coffee is arabica.

The trade-off is fragility. Arabica is vulnerable to coffee leaf rust and the coffee berry borer, and to rising temperatures. Its narrow genetic base, the result of a few seeds carried out of Ethiopia and Yemen centuries ago, makes breeding resistant varieties hard.

**In Indonesia:** arabica arrived with the VOC in 1699 and spread across the highlands of Java, then Sumatra, Sulawesi, Bali, Flores and Papua. Well-known varieties include Typica, S795, Sigarar Utang, Ateng and Gayo 1.

**Typical cup:** sweet, aromatic, with clear acidity; from chocolate and nuts to citrus, berries and flowers. Caffeine is about 1.2–1.5%.
