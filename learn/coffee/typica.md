Almost all arabica grown outside Ethiopia descends from a handful of plants taken from Yemen. One branch became **Typica**, the other **Bourbon**. Typica travelled from Yemen to India's Malabar coast, then with the VOC to Java in 1699, and from Java to Amsterdam, Paris and the Americas.

Typica trees are tall, with bronze-tipped young leaves and long, sloping branches. They give a clean, sweet, elegant cup but yield little and have no resistance to leaf rust, which is why most of Indonesia's lowland Typica was lost in the late 1800s.

**In Indonesia:** old Typica trees survive in parts of Papua (Wamena), Flores, Toraja and the Java highlands, often descended from colonial or missionary plantings.
