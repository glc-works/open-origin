title: Excelsa
crop: coffee

latin: Coffea liberica var. dewevrei
summary: Once its own species, now classed as a variety of Liberica, known for a tart, fruity cup and grown mostly in Southeast Asia.
---

Excelsa was described as a separate species, *Coffea excelsa*, in the early 1900s after it was found in Central Africa. In 2006 botanists reclassified it as a variety of liberica, *Coffea liberica* var. *dewevrei*. Many farmers and buyers still call it excelsa, because in the cup it behaves very differently from liberica.

The trees are large and vigorous, tolerate drought and disease, and grow at low to middle altitudes. Most of the world's small supply comes from Southeast Asia, especially Vietnam, the Philippines and Indonesia.

**In Indonesia:** excelsa is grown in parts of Java and Sumatra, often alongside robusta. Small producers have started processing it carefully for specialty buyers.

**Typical cup:** tart and fruity, with dark fruit, a winey or tamarind-like acidity and a lingering finish. Roasters often use it to add depth to blends.
