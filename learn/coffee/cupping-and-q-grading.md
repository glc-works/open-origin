**Cupping** is the standard way to taste and compare coffees. Freshly roasted, coarsely ground coffee is steeped in hot water in identical cups. Tasters break the crust that forms on top, smell it, skim it, and slurp the coffee from a spoon as it cools so they can judge it hot, warm and cool.

**A Q Grader** is a taster certified by the Coffee Quality Institute (CQI) to score arabica consistently with other graders around the world. The exam takes several days and covers smell, taste, cupping, green and roasted bean grading. **An R Grader** (sometimes called Q Robusta Grader) does the same for robusta, where 80+ points means "fine robusta".

### What gets scored

On the classic SCA/CQI cupping form, ten attributes are each scored up to 10 points, for a total out of 100:

| Attribute | What the taster asks |
|---|---|
| Fragrance / aroma | How does it smell, dry and then with water? |
| Flavour | How intense, complex and pleasant is the taste? |
| Aftertaste | Does the good taste last after swallowing? |
| Acidity | Is the brightness lively and pleasant, or sour? |
| Body | How does it feel in the mouth: thin, creamy, syrupy? |
| Balance | Do the parts fit together? |
| Uniformity | Do all five cups taste the same? |
| Clean cup | Are there any off flavours? |
| Sweetness | Is there a pleasant sweetness? |
| Overall | The taster's overall judgement |

Defects found in the cups are subtracted.

### The green beans count too

Before tasting, the grader inspects a 350 g sample of green coffee. For specialty grade it must have no primary (category 1) defects, such as fully black or sour beans or foreign matter, and no more than five full secondary defects, such as broken, insect-damaged or unripe beans. Moisture should be roughly 10–12%.

### What the numbers mean

| Score | Grade |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Outstanding |
| 85–89.99 | Excellent |
| 80–84.99 | Very good: specialty |
| Below 80 | Below specialty grade |

### A newer approach

In 2024 the Specialty Coffee Association introduced the **Coffee Value Assessment (CVA)**. It separates describing a coffee (what flavours are there and how strong) from judging it (how much the taster likes it), and adds the coffee's story and sustainability as part of its value. Both systems are in use today.

### On Open Origin

A score belongs to one lot from one harvest, not to a whole region. That is why scores go in a batch file next to a farm or group story, with `cupping_score` in its `quality` block and the name of whoever scored it in `tested_by`. We show them as they were reported.
