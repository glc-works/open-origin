---
title: Liberica
crop: coffee
type: species
latin: Coffea liberica var. liberica
summary: A tall tree with large, asymmetric beans from West Africa, grown in small amounts in Southeast Asia, including Jambi in Sumatra.
---

Liberica comes from West Africa and takes its name from Liberia. The trees can grow to 15–20 metres, and both the cherries and the beans are large and often lopsided. It makes up only a small share of the world's coffee, around 1–2%.

In the late 1800s, when leaf rust devastated arabica, liberica was planted across Southeast Asia as a replacement. Robusta later took its place almost everywhere, but liberica survived in pockets: the Philippines, where it is known as *kapeng barako*, Malaysia, and parts of Indonesia.

Liberica tolerates heat, low altitude and wet soils, and is drawing new interest as the climate warms.

**In Indonesia:** the best known is Kopi Liberika Tungkal Jambi, grown on peatland in Tanjung Jabung Barat, Jambi, which received Geographical Indication status. Liberica is also grown in Bengkulu, Riau and the Meranti Islands.

**Typical cup:** unusual and polarising; jackfruit, smoky wood, dark fruit and a hint of floral, with a heavy body.
