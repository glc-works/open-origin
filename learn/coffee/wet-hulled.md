---
title: Wet-hulled (giling basah)
crop: coffee
type: process
summary: Indonesia's signature process, where the parchment is hulled off while the bean is still wet, giving heavy body and earthy, spicy notes.
---

Wet hulling, *giling basah* in Indonesian, is rare outside Indonesia. It is the reason Sumatran coffee tastes the way it does.

1. Farmers pulp the cherries at home and leave the beans in sacks or buckets overnight to ferment lightly, then wash them.
2. The beans in their parchment are dried for only a short time, to about 30–50% moisture, and sold to a collector, often within a day or two.
3. The collector removes the parchment while the bean is still wet and soft, then dries the green bean to about 12%.

Hulling early lets farmers get paid quickly and suits the humid climate, where slow drying is difficult. The wet bean is exposed and dries fast, which gives the typical dark blue-green colour, heavy body, low acidity, and earthy, woody, herbal and spicy notes.

**Where:** Aceh (Gayo), North Sumatra (Mandailing, Lintong, Sidikalang), and Sulawesi (Toraja, Kalosi).
